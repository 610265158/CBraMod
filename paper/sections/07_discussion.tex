\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{What can be concluded about EEG foundation models?}

Full-fine-tuning accuracy alone does not identify the source of a foundation
model's gains. Our controls show that a compact generic encoder can be highly
competitive after geometry alignment, while REVE remains ahead on TUAB and
Mumtaz2016 and CBraMod remains ahead on SEED-V and on the SHU-MI PR-AUC, which
rules out universal replacement. Visual pretraining improves balanced accuracy
in 33 of 36 matched backbone--dataset comparisons under the same five-seed,
validation-selected protocol (34 of 36 under the primary validation metric),
with exceptions for EfficientNet-B0 on SEED-V and Mumtaz2016 and
ConvNeXt-Tiny on TUAB under balanced accuracy. Thus the gain reflects
transferable structure in the visual weights, not only a favorable
architecture. Geometry, backbone,
task, and optimization still matter, so EEG foundation models should be
compared against strong architecture-and-geometry controls.

Vision pretraining is therefore a \emph{conditional} free lunch in a strong
practical sense: it provides a broadly reusable prior that wins or remains
closely competitive on most benchmarks without EEG-specific pretraining. Its
value still depends on representation geometry, backbone, and task, so this
does not imply that EEG pretraining is unnecessary in frozen, few-shot, or
cross-montage settings. Rather, our results provide a design reference for
EEG-specific pretraining and downstream transfer: future EEG models should
quantify gains beyond a geometry-aligned visual prior, especially for few-shot
adaptation, frozen representations, or cross-montage generalization.

\subsection{Limitations}

Published EEG baselines are contextual references rather than paired local
reruns, although the compared rows use inherited benchmark partitions. Our
experiments use dataset-specific recipes; frozen probing covers two visual
backbones, while few-shot transfer, missing-channel and cross-montage robustness, pretraining
efficiency, and paired significance tests remain open. Channel count is
confounded with montage and task, so folding should not be interpreted as a
causal effect of electrode density.

\subsection{Broader impact}

Compact backbones may lower hardware barriers, but benchmark accuracy is not
clinical readiness. Deployment requires external validation, calibration,
subgroup analysis, and human oversight. Because folding is invertible, it
provides no privacy protection.
