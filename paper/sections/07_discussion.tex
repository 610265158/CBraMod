\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{What can be concluded about EEG foundation models?}

The study is designed around a narrow but important question: full fine-tuning
performance alone may not identify the source of a foundation model's gains.
The corrected phase-interleaved benchmark shows that a compact generic encoder
can be competitive on several tasks, while the deficits on FACED, TUAB,
SEED-V, SHU-MI, and Mumtaz2016 reject a universal replacement claim. More generally, new
foundation models should compare against strong architecture-and-geometry
controls, not only task-specific networks with weaker transfer recipes.
This interpretation is consistent with recent calls to reassess EEG
foundation models against carefully tuned conventional decoders
\citep{yang2026eegworth}.  The shared conclusion is methodological: stronger
controls are needed before an improvement can be attributed specifically to
large-scale EEG pretraining; it is not evidence that foundation models are
categorically unnecessary.

The expanded comparison also sharpens the role of scale and spatial identity.
Against 69M-parameter \reve{}-Base, \method{} is higher on four of eight shared
primary metrics, but two of those comparisons carry TUEV-version or
ISRUC-preprocessing caveats. The clean PhysioNet-MI and BCIC2020-3 gains show
that large-scale EEG pretraining is not always necessary under full
fine-tuning. The TUAB and FACED deficits show the converse: physical
coordinates and heterogeneous EEG pretraining remain valuable when montage or
subject shift dominates.

In the terminology of our title, vision pretraining is a \emph{conditional}
free lunch: its weights are available without additional EEG pretraining, but
their value depends on input geometry, downstream optimization, and task
characteristics. Six gains and five deficits across the 11
benchmarks make this conditionality empirically visible.

The results do \emph{not} show that EEG pretraining is unnecessary.  This work
does not yet evaluate few-shot learning, missing-channel robustness, frozen
features, cross-montage transfer, or pretraining-data efficiency.  These are
precisely the settings in which large, coordinate-aware EEG models should be
most valuable.  A fair interpretation is that generic visual priors are an
unexpectedly strong component under end-to-end supervised adaptation.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline comparability.}  BIOT, LaBraM, \cbramod{}, and
    \reve{} are published references, not local paired reproductions. The
    manifests follow shared benchmark partitions, but preprocessing,
    optimization, seeds, and model selection remain method-specific. TUEV is a
    cross-version comparison (local v2.0.1 versus published v2.0.0), and
    REVE--ISRUC uses corrected channel selection. For TUAB and TUEV we use
    \cbramod{}'s target-corpus-excluded controls rather than its full-pretraining
    results.
    \item \textbf{Development-set contamination.}  Formal runs evaluate the
    test set once, but some candidate recipes were inspired by earlier test-
    visible exploration.  A prospectively frozen rerun is needed for the final
    paper.
    \item \textbf{Dataset-specific recipes.}  The encoder is fixed, but $P$,
    optimization, input scale, and augmentation vary by dataset.  This is a
    tuned downstream protocol, not zero-shot universality.  We introduce the
    prespecified geometry rule in Eq.~\ref{eq:p-geometry-rule} to reduce this
    freedom. The unified geometry confirmations are complete, while SEED-V
    remains a measured counterexample to shape-only selection.
    \item \textbf{Incomplete controls.} Random initialization, matched
    supervised architectures,
    identical-pipeline foundation baselines, FLOPs, throughput, and memory are
    not yet in the main table.
    \item \textbf{Statistical uncertainty.}  Three seeds are informative but
    limited, especially for MentalArithmetic.  Subject-level bootstrap
    intervals and paired tests would better reflect evaluation uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Broader impact}

A small vision backbone may reduce the hardware barrier for EEG research and
deployment.  However, high benchmark performance does not imply clinical
readiness.  EEG datasets encode acquisition-site, demographic, device, and
labeling biases; a model can exploit these shortcuts.  Clinical use would
require external validation, calibration, uncertainty reporting, subgroup
analysis, and human oversight.  The invertibility of folding also means it does
not provide privacy protection: identifiable or sensitive waveform information
remains present in the transformed input.
