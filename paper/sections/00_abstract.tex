\begin{abstract}
EEG foundation models combine domain-specific architectures with large-scale
self-supervised pretraining. We ask whether visual representations learned
from natural images can transfer to EEG, and whether EEG-specific pretraining
provides a consistent advantage under full supervised fine-tuning. We introduce
\method{}, a parameter-free bijective temporal-folding interface that maps an
EEG matrix from $C\times T$ to $(CP)\times(T/P)$. Folding preserves every sample
while compensating for the spatial sparsity of low-channel EEG and exposing
phase-aware local neighborhoods to a 2D visual backbone. We evaluate
ImageNet-initialized EfficientNet-B0 and DINOv3-initialized ConvNeXt-Tiny and
ViT-Small across a 12-dataset suite. The completed results show that visual
pretraining transfers across architectures and can match or exceed published
supervised EEG models and EEG foundation models on several tasks, while
foundation models retain advantages on others. These findings establish
representation geometry as a necessary control when assessing EEG-specific
pretraining and show that cross-domain transfer does not require semantic
similarity between the source and target modalities.
\end{abstract}
