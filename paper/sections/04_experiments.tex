\subsection{Datasets and splits}

affective computing, motor imagery, imagined speech, depression detection,
and mental workload. Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes their task families,
input shapes, and label spaces. All recordings are represented at 200\,Hz.
Regular inputs follow the $[B,C,T]$ convention; ISRUC retains a sequence of 20
labelled epochs per record.

For all datasets, we inherit the record construction, preprocessing order, and
public train/validation/test partitions used by the CBraMod and REVE benchmarks
\citep{wang2024cbramod,elouahidi2025reve}. TUEV is the only version caveat: we
use v2.0.1 rather than v2.0.0. The releases share the official 29,421-sample
test set, but their training and validation counts differ. Exact split counts
and partition rules for all 12 datasets are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:splits} (Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results}).

\subsection{Metrics and model selection}

Binary tasks report balanced accuracy (BA), PR-AUC, and ROC-AUC; multiclass
tasks report BA, Cohen's $\kappa$, and weighted F1. Validation PR-AUC and
validation $\kappa$ are the checkpoint-selection metrics for binary and
multiclass tasks, respectively. The remaining metrics are used only for final
reporting. Each recipe is trained with seeds 42--46. For every seed, we select
one checkpoint using validation performance and evaluate it once on the test
set; tables report the arithmetic mean and population standard deviation.

We compare against published task-specific models and EEG foundation models
under the inherited benchmark partitions. For CBraMod on TUAB and TUEV, we
use variants whose pretraining excludes the target corpus. We additionally
rerun released CBraMod and REVE models on TUAB, FACED, and HMC as a
supplementary fairness check; full protocols and results are provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:foundation-rerun}.

\subsection{Models and training}

We use ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B0 and DINOv3-pretrained
ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small with one-channel inputs. Their original
classification layers are replaced by task-specific prediction heads, and the
encoder and head are fine-tuned jointly. Frozen-transfer experiments keep the
encoder fixed and train only the prediction head. Backbone-specific feature
aggregation and head definitions are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:recipes}.

All models use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled} with cosine decay,
bfloat16 mixed precision, and dropout 0.1. The default batch size is 32, with
16 for ISRUC and 64 for MentalArithmetic. Signals are clipped and scaled
before folding, and the fold factor is fixed using
Eq.~\ref{eq:p-geometry-rule}. Dataset-specific optimization settings and
preprocessing details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:recipes}.

\subsection{Ablation and control design}

Five studies isolate the sources of performance. \textbf{Cross-backbone
evaluation} tests whether the interface transfers across convolutional and
Transformer encoders. \textbf{Matched initialization controls} compare
pretrained and randomly initialized versions of the same model under an
otherwise identical recipe. \textbf{Frozen linear probing} tests transfer
without updating the encoder. \textbf{Folding-layout controls} compare
temporal folding with a contiguous reshape that preserves the same
samples and output shape. Finally, \textbf{fold-factor sweeps} over
$P\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ measure sensitivity to the induced geometry. Complete
settings and results are reported in Sections~\ref{sec:pretraining-ablation}--
\ref{sec:fold-factor-sensitivity} and the appendix.
