\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Task-specific decoders and EEG foundation models}

EEGNet uses compact temporal and depthwise spatial convolutions tailored to
brain--computer interfaces \citep{lawhern2018eegnet}.  DeepConvNet and
ShallowConvNet similarly encode useful signal-processing priors through
temporal filters, spatial filters, and pooling \citep{schirrmeister2017deep}.
These networks establish that convolution is a natural EEG primitive, but they
typically assume a fixed montage and are optimized within one benchmark.

\biot{} maps heterogeneous biosignals to frequency-domain tokens and trains
across datasets \citep{yang2024biot}.  \labram{} uses masked EEG modeling at
large scale \citep{jiang2024labram}.  \cbramod{} combines time-domain patches,
Fourier magnitude, and criss-cross attention in a compact encoder
\citep{wang2024cbramod}.  \reve{} scales pretraining to tens of thousands of
subjects and uses physical electrode coordinates to support unseen montages
\citep{elouahidi2025reve}.  These methods primarily study the value of
EEG-domain pretraining.  We instead hold domain pretraining absent and ask how
much performance follows from geometry plus a mature generic backbone.

Recent work has also explicitly questioned whether EEG foundation models
consistently justify their added complexity when compared with carefully tuned
traditional decoders across diverse BCI tasks \citep{yang2026eegworth}.  This
critique motivates stronger controls, but does not imply that EEG pretraining
is uniformly ineffective.  Our study tests a complementary control: a compact
generic encoder with no EEG pretraining, paired with a lossless geometry
adapter and evaluated across 11 tasks.

\subsection{Vision transfer and lossless rearrangement}

ImageNet pretraining provides transferable low- and mid-level filters
\citep{deng2009imagenet}.  EfficientNet jointly scales network width, depth,
and image resolution \citep{tan2019efficientnet}; vision transformers provide
an alternative patch-token formulation \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit}.  Generic
vision models have been applied to spectrograms and topographic EEG maps, but
these representations introduce a Fourier transform, interpolation, or an
electrode layout.  Our input remains the raw waveform and changes indices only.

Pixel shuffle and space-to-depth operations demonstrate that a tensor
reindexing can substantially alter the computation performed by a fixed
convolutional architecture \citep{shi2016realtime}.  Temporal folding is
related in spirit but is asymmetric: it trades temporal width for a joint
electrode--phase height.  Each folded column contains $P$ consecutive samples,
while each row follows one phase at stride $P$.  This explicit signal meaning
lets us analyze the original temporal offsets represented by local 2D
neighbors.

\subsection{Positioning of this work}

The closest empirical references are \cbramod{} and \reve{}, because their
benchmark suites supply the subject- or trial-level partitions used here.
They also expose two complementary EEG priors: \cbramod{} models temporal and
cross-channel structure with a compact criss-cross transformer, whereas
\reve{} uses physical electrode coordinates and 69M parameters pretrained on
92 datasets. Our question is different from proposing another EEG foundation
model. We ask how far a 4.01M-parameter ImageNet encoder can go after only a
lossless change of input geometry. This comparison separates three possible
sources of downstream performance---task architecture, EEG pretraining, and
representation geometry---that are otherwise easy to conflate.
