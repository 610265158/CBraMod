The expanded table supports a more precise claim than a single win count. The
REVE rows are transcribed from its published benchmark tables
\citep{elouahidi2025reve}; they provide an additional EEG-foundation-model
reference on the eight overlapping tasks.
EfficientNet-B0 exceeds published \cbramod{} on six primary metrics and is
lower on five. The largest gains are BCIC2020-3 (+15.90 points),
MentalArithmetic (+10.06), and CHB-MIT (+4.03). It is also the best displayed
method on the primary metric for CHB-MIT, TUEV, ISRUC, PhysioNet-MI, and
BCIC2020-3, although TUEV is cross-version and REVE--ISRUC uses different
channel preprocessing. Among the eight tasks shared with \reve{}, \method{} is
higher on four (TUEV, ISRUC, PhysioNet-MI, and BCIC2020-3) and lower on four
(TUAB, FACED, Mumtaz2016, and MentalArithmetic). The cleanest gains over the
69M-parameter REVE-Base are therefore PhysioNet-MI and BCIC2020-3. Conversely,
TUAB and FACED show higher reported values for the large-scale, coordinate-aware
EEG-pretrained references.
For completeness, the primary-metric differences relative to REVE-Base are
$-5.78$, $+1.18$, $+2.05$, $-13.43$, $+1.09$, $+12.63$, $-3.92$, and $-1.97$
percentage points for TUAB, TUEV, ISRUC, FACED, PhysioNet-MI, BCIC2020-3,
Mumtaz2016, and MentalArithmetic, respectively. Both metrics and their
reported standard deviations are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results}.

largest failure relative to \cbramod{} (FACED).

\paragraph{Stable folded-input gain: ISRUC.}
All five kappa values (.7729, .7710, .7747, .7672, and .7665) exceed the
published .7442 reference, with population standard deviation .0032. ISRUC uses
$P=12$ and retains 20 consecutive sleep epochs, showing that the adapter also
works on a multi-epoch record with per-epoch classification.

\paragraph{Seed-sensitive folded-input gain: MentalArithmetic.}
The model improves PR-AUC by 10.06 points and AUROC by 10.22 points over
\cbramod{}, but its PR-AUC standard deviation is .0419. This task therefore
supports the attainable mean while also exposing uncertainty on a small,
imbalanced dataset.

\paragraph{Unfolded-input control: PhysioNet-MI.}
PhysioNet-MI already has 64 channels and uses $P=1$. Its gain shows that
folding is not forced when the native geometry is compatible with the CNN, and
that part of the result comes from the transferred visual encoder itself.

\paragraph{Boundary case: FACED.}
FACED remains 13.04 kappa points below \cbramod{}. The contrast with the gains
above suggests that fixed row order and generic visual locality are
insufficient when subject shift or physical electrode topology dominates.

\subsection{Strong supervised controls}

Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results} expands all 11 datasets with the complete
shared-benchmark supervised suite reported by \cbramod{} and \reve{}. On the
primary metric, EfficientNet-B0 exceeds the strongest displayed supervised
baseline on nine datasets; FACED and Mumtaz2016 are the exceptions. TUAB is
representative: the visual model exceeds all seven supervised baselines in
PR-AUC, but remains below every displayed EEG foundation model. This separates
two claims that a single headline table can obscure: geometry-aligned vision
transfer is a strong supervised architecture baseline, while EEG pretraining
still provides a clear advantage on selected tasks.

\subsection{Stability and model scale}

The standard deviations reveal two regimes. ISRUC (.0032) and TUAB (.0033)
are the most stable primary-metric results, followed by SHU-MI (.0178) and
TUEV (.0190). CHB-MIT (.0536) and MentalArithmetic (.0419) remain the most
seed-sensitive.
MentalArithmetic has only 1,343
training examples and 172 validation examples, including a roughly 3:1 class
imbalance.  Weighted BCE, moderate time roll and mirror augmentation, and
EMA reduce its seed sensitivity without balanced resampling.  The reported
.7273 PR-AUC is the validation-selected three-seed mean, not an exploratory
best-seed or best-epoch value.

\paragraph{Model scale.}

After removing the unused ImageNet classifier, EfficientNet-B0 contains
approximately 4.01M encoder parameters, plus a small task head.  This is close
to the reported 4.0M parameters of \cbramod{}, but our model uses no EEG
self-supervised pretraining. \reve{}-Base is substantially larger and is
pretrained on a much broader EEG corpus.  The comparison therefore isolates a
useful point: at compact scale and under full fine-tuning, input geometry and
generic convolutional features can account for a large fraction of downstream
performance.  It does not address frozen transfer, where large EEG foundation
models are expected to retain an advantage.
