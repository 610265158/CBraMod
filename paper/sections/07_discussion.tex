\section{Discussion and Limitations}

We revisit our three contributions in light of the matched controls and the
benchmark comparison. For the
lossless interface, the folding-layout controls show that the reindexing itself
matters: temporal folding beats a contiguous reshape of the same samples in 25
of 27 selection-metric comparisons, so ordering, not height alone, carries part
of the gain, and the fold-factor rule remains a reproducible prior, not a
universal optimum.

For the transfer evidence, the gains hold across convolutional and Transformer
backbones alike. Two controls show that the pretrained representation itself
matters: with random initialization, the selection metric falls in 34 of 36
backbone--dataset comparisons (BA: 33 of 36); and a linear probe on frozen
DINOv3 features still matches the published non-pooling CBraMod probe on
several tasks, though not REVE-Base.

For the benchmark comparison, at least one visual backbone exceeds the
strongest EEG foundation reference on both main metrics in 8 of
the 12 tasks, while published references remain ahead on TUAB and Mumtaz2016
(REVE) and on SEED-V and the SHU-MI PR-AUC (CBraMod); visual transfer is
therefore competitive but not a replacement.

Taken together, vision pretraining is a \emph{conditional} free lunch: a
broadly reusable prior that wins or stays closely competitive on most
benchmarks without any EEG-specific pretraining. This matters practically in EEG, where
pretraining corpora, hardware, and compute are expensive; a compact ImageNet-
or DINOv3-initialized backbone with a parameter-free adapter already provides a
strong and reproducible baseline, although benchmark accuracy is not clinical
readiness. It also offers a new perspective on EEG foundation models: gains
attributed to EEG-specific pretraining should be measured against a
geometry-aligned visual control that shares the data, splits, and protocol.
This does not mean that EEG pretraining is unnecessary; our frozen probes still
trail REVE-Base on several tasks, and few-shot or cross-montage transfer may
favor EEG-specific representations.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. Most EEG baselines remain
contextual published references rather than paired local reruns;
Appendix~\ref{app:foundation-rerun} adds matched-pipeline reruns for five
datasets, but these do not reproduce every original training stage, and the
headline counts select the best of three backbones.
Frozen probing covers two visual backbones, and channel count is confounded
with montage and task. Future work should therefore address automatic
fold-factor selection (validation-based or learned), channel-order and
missing-channel robustness, few-shot and cross-montage transfer,
pretraining-efficiency comparisons, and paired significance tests.
