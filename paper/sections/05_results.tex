\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} summarizes the corrected phase-interleaved
results.  EfficientNet-B0 improves the primary metric over the published
\cbramod{} reference on six datasets (CHB-MIT, TUEV, ISRUC, PhysioNet-MI,
BCIC2020-3, and MentalArithmetic), is effectively tied on SEED-V, and is lower
on TUAB, SHU-MI, Mumtaz2016, and FACED.  These comparisons use published
CBraMod references rather than same-pipeline reruns, so they should be read as
evidence of competitiveness rather than a definitive paired ranking.

\paragraph{Reliable gains.}
TUEV is the clearest confirmation that the result is not one favorable seed:
all three $\kappa$ values (.6871, .7566, and .7053) exceed the .6744 reference,
and the mean F1 also improves.  ISRUC, CHB-MIT, and BCIC2020-3 provide further
primary-metric gains of 3.46, 7.37, and 9.23 percentage points, respectively.
ISRUC combines P=12 with warmup, EMA, and time roll; its three kappa values
(.7810, .7784, and .7771) are all above the .7442 reference, with population
standard deviation .0016.
PhysioNet-MI improves by 1.11 kappa points and .71 F1 points.  Its warmup and
EMA recipe reduces kappa standard deviation from .0125 in the superseded P=1
run to .0038, while all three seeds exceed the published kappa reference.
MentalArithmetic gains 8.81 PR-AUC points and 4.65 AUROC points.  Its weighted
BCE stability recipe reduces the PR-AUC standard deviation from .0649 in the
superseded recipe to .0364, while AUROC standard deviation falls from .0303 to
.0040.

\paragraph{Near parity.}
SEED-V is at parity (mean $\kappa$ .2565 versus .2569, with a slightly higher
F1), which is why we report the three-seed mean rather than a best-seed value.
PhysioNet-MI's gain uses $P=1$, showing that folding is not forced when the
unfolded 64-row geometry is already compatible with the CNN.

\paragraph{Where generic geometry is insufficient.}
FACED remains the largest gap, with a 13.71-point $\kappa$ deficit.  The result
cannot be explained by model size alone because the same encoder is competitive
on another emotion dataset, SEED-V.  FACED may depend more strongly on subject
shift, amplitude distribution, physical electrode topology, or spatially
distributed affective patterns.  Mumtaz2016 is less diagnostic because both
methods operate near the ceiling, but \cbramod{} remains consistently higher.

\subsection{Result stability}

The standard deviations reveal two regimes.  ISRUC is the most stable
multiclass result (kappa standard deviation .0016), and Mumtaz2016, TUAB,
SEED-V, and MentalArithmetic AUROC are also comparatively stable, while
TUEV, FACED, and SHU-MI are more sensitive.  MentalArithmetic has only 1,343
training examples and 172 validation examples, including a roughly 3:1 class
imbalance.  Weighted BCE, moderate time roll and mirror augmentation, and
EMA reduce its seed sensitivity without balanced resampling.  The reported
.7148 PR-AUC is the validation-selected three-seed mean, not an exploratory
best-seed or best-epoch value.

\subsection{Parameter efficiency}

After removing the unused ImageNet classifier, EfficientNet-B0 contains
approximately 4.01M encoder parameters, plus a small task head.  This is close
to the reported 4.9M parameters of \cbramod{}, but our model uses no EEG
self-supervised pretraining.  \reve{}-Base is substantially larger and is
pretrained on a much broader EEG corpus.  The comparison therefore isolates a
useful point: at compact scale and under full fine-tuning, input geometry and
generic convolutional features can account for a large fraction of downstream
performance.  It does not address frozen transfer, where large EEG foundation
models are expected to retain an advantage.
