\section{Conclusion}

We introduced phase-interleaved, lossless temporal folding as a parameter-free
interface between EEG and generic vision backbones. Across 12 benchmarks, it
makes mature visual representations competitive under full fine-tuning, and the
matched controls show where that competitiveness comes from: visual pretraining
improves the selection metric in 34 of 36 backbone--dataset comparisons (BA: 33
of 36), and folding beats a contiguous reshape in 25 of 27 selection-metric
comparisons. The gain therefore requires both the visual weights and the
geometry. EEG-specific models nonetheless remain stronger on some tasks.

The practical principle is to align signal geometry and measure the value of
existing representations before assuming that larger EEG-specific pretraining
is necessary for downstream gains.
