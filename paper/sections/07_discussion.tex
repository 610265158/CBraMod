\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{What can be concluded about EEG foundation models?}

The study is designed around a narrow question: full fine-tuning performance
alone may not identify the source of a foundation model's gains. The corrected
benchmark shows that a compact generic encoder is competitive on several
tasks, while primary-metric deficits on TUAB, SEED-V, SHU-MI, and Mumtaz2016
reject a universal replacement claim; the retained supervised and foundation
baselines make the comparison a ladder of controls, not a winner-take-all
leaderboard. New foundation models should therefore be compared against strong
architecture-and-geometry controls, not only task-specific networks with
weaker transfer recipes, consistent with recent calls to reassess EEG
foundation models \citep{yang2026eegworth}. The conclusion is methodological:
stronger controls are needed before an improvement can be attributed
specifically to large-scale EEG pretraining, which is not evidence that
foundation models are categorically unnecessary.

The matched initialization ablation isolates one part of this claim. Across
TUEV, PhysioNet-MI, ISRUC, and SHU-MI, random initialization lowers balanced
accuracy in 11 of 12 backbone--dataset pairs by 0.4--24.6 points under the
same five-seed, validation-selected protocol; the one exception (SHU-MI with
ViT-Small) is within one point, and the pretraining benefit otherwise grows
with model capacity, from +0.4 points for EfficientNet-B0 on the largest
training set to +24.6 points for ConvNeXt-Tiny on TUEV. Thus the pretrained
visual representation itself supplies transferable structure for EEG
fine-tuning, not merely a workable architecture; whether EEG pretraining is
better for frozen, few-shot, unseen-montage, or topology-sensitive transfer
remains open.

The comparison also sharpens the role of scale and spatial resolution. Several
64-channel tasks remain competitive at $P=1$, whereas low-channel recordings
benefit from folding, which preserves signal-bearing height and exposes
phase-structured neighborhoods; channel count is nevertheless confounded with
montage, dataset, and task, so denser electrodes are a testable direction
rather than a guaranteed improvement. Against 69M-parameter \reve{}-Base,
\method{} is competitive on selected shared tasks, and the common held-out
partitions make the comparison directly interpretable. PhysioNet-MI and
BCIC2020-3 show that large-scale EEG pretraining is not always necessary under
full fine-tuning, while FACED favors ConvNeXt-Tiny over both EEG references,
and TUAB stays close; these outcomes show task and architecture dependence
without isolating physical coordinates or EEG pretraining.

In the terminology of our title, vision pretraining is a \emph{conditional}
free lunch: its weights are available without EEG pretraining, but their value
depends on input geometry, downstream optimization, and task characteristics,
and the mixed pattern across the 12 benchmarks makes this conditionality
visible. The results do \emph{not} show that EEG pretraining is unnecessary:
few-shot learning, missing-channel robustness, frozen features, cross-montage
transfer, and pretraining-data efficiency are untested, and these are
precisely the settings where large coordinate-aware EEG models should be most
valuable.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Protocol alignment.} EEGNet, SPaRCNet, BIOT, LaBraM,
    \cbramod{}, and \reve{} are published results rather than local paired
    reproductions. Those rows share our subject- or trial-level partitions,
    which are inherited from the public \cbramod{} and \reve{} code
    \citep{wang2024cbramod,elouahidi2025reve}; the remaining rows are
    transcribed contextual references. The local TUEV release differs only in
    its training and validation segments, with an unchanged held-out test
    set, and TUAB/TUEV use \cbramod{}'s target-corpus-excluded controls.
    Preprocessing, optimization, seeds, and model selection remain
    method-specific.
    \item \textbf{Dataset-specific recipes.} The encoder is fixed, but
    optimization, input scale, and augmentation were recipe-searched per
    dataset, while $P$ follows the prespecified rule of
    Eq.~\ref{eq:p-geometry-rule}. This is a tuned downstream protocol, not
    zero-shot universality, and the selected factors are not claimed to be
    optimal.
    \item \textbf{Remaining controls.} Random-initialization controls cover
    TUEV, PhysioNet-MI, ISRUC, and SHU-MI, and parameters and FLOPs are
    reported in Table~\ref{tab:model-scale}; frozen transfer and
    same-pipeline EEG-foundation reruns remain for future work.
    \item \textbf{Statistical uncertainty.} We report population standard
    deviations over five seeds but no paired significance tests;
    subject-level bootstrap intervals would better reflect evaluation
    uncertainty, especially for MentalArithmetic.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Broader impact}

A small vision backbone may reduce the hardware barrier for EEG research and
deployment, but high benchmark performance does not imply clinical readiness:
EEG datasets encode acquisition-site, demographic, device, and labeling biases
that a model can exploit. Clinical use would require external validation,
calibration, uncertainty reporting, subgroup analysis, and human oversight.
The invertibility of folding also means it provides no privacy protection,
since identifiable waveform information remains in the transformed input.
