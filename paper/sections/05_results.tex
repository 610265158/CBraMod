The unified table shows a heterogeneous but systematic pattern. ConvNeXt-Tiny
exceeds every included EEG foundation-model reference on both main metrics in
8 datasets. The strongest visual backbone varies across tasks: ViT-Small
leads on CHB-MIT, TUEV, and BCIC2020-3; ConvNeXt-Tiny leads on ISRUC, HMC,
FACED, and PhysioNet-MI; and EfficientNet-B0 leads on MentalArithmetic.
These results show that the interface transfers across both CNN and Transformer
backbones, while backbone choice remains task-dependent.

The comparison also defines clear limits. Among the included references, REVE remains strongest on TUAB and
Mumtaz2016, while CBraMod leads on SEED-V and on the SHU-MI PR-AUC. Visual
transfer is therefore competitive but not a replacement. PhysioNet-MI further
shows that competitive transfer can occur at $P=1$, without folding. The TUEV release difference leaves the held-out test
set unchanged. Published foundation-model rows are contextual references
rather than local reruns. As a robustness check, we also reran CBraMod and
REVE under the matched pipeline on all twelve datasets; under this common
protocol at least one vision backbone exceeds both reruns on balanced
accuracy and the selection metric in
eight, the exceptions being SHU-MI, Mumtaz2016, PhysioNet-MI, and SEED-V,
where REVE-Base leads. Complete five-seed results for all
24 model--dataset pairs and the protocol caveats are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:foundation-rerun}.

Across the complete 36 matched backbone--dataset comparisons, pretraining
improves the selection metric in 34 cases and balanced accuracy in 33;
complete values are in
Appendix~\ref{app:complete-ablations}. Under the selection metric the
exceptions are EfficientNet-B0 on SEED-V and ISRUC; under balanced accuracy
they are EfficientNet-B0 on SEED-V and Mumtaz2016 and ConvNeXt-Tiny on TUAB. The
representative rows show large gains on TUEV and PhysioNet-MI, a small gain on
ISRUC, and a
backbone-dependent result on SHU-MI. The magnitude is therefore task- and
backbone-dependent. The magnitude of the gain varies substantially across datasets, with the
largest improvements appearing on FACED, BCIC2020-3, and MentalArithmetic. Thus generic visual
representations provide a reusable initialization for EEG fine-tuning after
geometry alignment. This is evidence for transfer from visual pretraining to
EEG, not evidence that EEG pretraining is unnecessary in frozen, few-shot, or
cross-montage settings.

\subsection{Frozen transfer and linear probing}
\label{sec:linear-probe}

We freeze the DINOv3-initialized ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small encoders and
train only a linear task head to test direct representation transfer.
EfficientNet-B0 is excluded because its BatchNorm running statistics introduce
an additional choice between freezing and updating state.
Table~\ref{tab:linear-probe-representative} reports representative BA results;
Appendix~\ref{app:linear-probe} provides the complete 12-dataset comparison
against the published non-pooling references.

Frozen ConvNeXt exceeds the published non-pooling CBraMod probe in balanced
accuracy on TUAB,
ISRUC, and MentalArithmetic, but remains below REVE-Base on those tasks.
FACED is backbone-sensitive, and both visual probes trail the published
references on BCIC2020-3. These results support task-dependent transfer from
visual representations without encoder updates, rather than universal
superiority or equivalence to EEG-specific pretraining. Linear probing alone
does not isolate the contribution of pretraining from architecture; the
matched initialization controls in Section~\ref{sec:pretraining-ablation}
address that question under full fine-tuning.

\subsection{Temporal folding versus contiguous reshape}
\label{sec:fold-reshape}

To isolate temporal adjacency from simple height expansion, we compare
temporal folding with a contiguous-chunk reshape. Both produce the
same $CP\times(T/P)$ tensor and retain the same samples; only their time-to-row
mapping differs. The control applies to the nine datasets with $P>1$ (folding
reduces to the identity for the other three), i.e., 27 of the 36
backbone--dataset pairs: temporal folding is favored by the selection metric in 25 of them (17 of 18
for the two CNN backbones and 8 of 9 for ViT-Small), with small reversals on
SHU-MI for EfficientNet-B0 and on CHB-MIT for ViT-Small. Across these 27 pairs,
balanced accuracy improves by an average of 4.64 percentage points (median: 3.89 points;
range: $-4.09$ to $+22.85$ points). The clearest gains
occur on FACED and MentalArithmetic, with smaller but consistent advantages on
the clinical and sleep tasks; ViT-Small shows its largest gains on SHU-MI and
HMC. Table~\ref{tab:fold-representative} shows representative balanced-accuracy
values for the two CNN backbones; complete results are in
Appendix~\ref{app:fold-reshape}. Thus the control isolates sample ordering,
not merely the increase in input height.

\subsection{Fold-factor sensitivity}
\label{sec:fold-factor-sensitivity}

The fold factor is tunable. We sweep $P\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ on CHB-MIT, TUEV,
and MentalArithmetic across all three backbones. The geometry-based rule
usually selects a factor near the best-performing region in these sweeps,
although the preferred value varies with the dataset, backbone, and metric.
We therefore use the rule as a reproducible geometric prior rather than a
universal optimum; validation-based tuning may provide additional gains.
Complete results are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:p-ablation}, and any tuned
factor uses validation data only.
