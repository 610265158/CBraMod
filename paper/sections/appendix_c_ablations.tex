Four controls isolate the sources of performance, following the design in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; all four reuse the splits, seeds, and validation
protocol of the main experiments. \textbf{Matched initialization} repeats each
recipe with a randomly initialized backbone: visual pretraining improves the
selection metric in 34 of the 36 dataset--backbone comparisons and balanced
accuracy in 33 (PR-AUC for binary tasks, $\kappa$ for multiclass tasks).
\textbf{Frozen linear probing} updates only a linear head, and the
resulting frozen DINOv3 features remain competitive with the published
non-pooling CBraMod probe on several tasks but stay below REVE-Base.
\textbf{Folding-layout controls} replace temporal folding with a contiguous
reshape of the same samples, and the comparison favors folding in 25 of 27
selection-metric cases. \textbf{Fold-factor sweeps} over $P\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ show that the
preferred factor depends on the task and the backbone, while the geometry rule
of Section~\ref{sec:p-selection} provides a reproducible near-optimal default.
All values are final test means $\pm$ population standard deviations over five
seeds. In every table, $\Delta$ is reported in percentage points and compares
the first condition against the second (pretrained versus random, folding
versus contiguous reshape).

The temporal-folding control shows a consistent advantage for CNN backbones across
most datasets, with the largest differences on FACED and MentalArithmetic and
smaller but positive differences on clinical and sleep tasks (the only CNN
exception is a 0.3pp SHU-MI reversal for EfficientNet-B0). ViT-Small is more
task-sensitive: it benefits clearly on TUEV, HMC, and SHU-MI, but contiguous
reshape is better on CHB-MIT and nearly tied on FACED and MentalArithmetic.
Thus the gain is not explained by height expansion alone; it depends on how the
backbone uses the phase-structured neighborhoods.

The sweeps confirm that the preferred factor is task- and backbone-dependent.
On MentalArithmetic, EfficientNet-B0 peaks at $P=4$ whereas both DINOv3
backbones peak at $P=2$ on PR-AUC. On CHB-MIT, PR-AUC is best at $P=4$ for
EfficientNet-B0 and ViT-Small but at $P=1$ for ConvNeXt-Tiny, and
EfficientNet-B0 reaches its highest BA at $P=8$ without improving its
validation-selected metric. On TUEV, $\kappa$ and weighted F1 are best at
$P=8$ for ConvNeXt-Tiny and at $P=4$ for ViT-Small, while EfficientNet-B0 is
nearly tied between $P=2$ and $P=4$ ($\kappa=.6907$ vs $.6901$). The geometry
rule therefore serves as a reproducible default; validation-based tuning can
improve the result when additional compute is available.
