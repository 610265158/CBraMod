The unified table shows a heterogeneous but systematic pattern. At least one
visual backbone exceeds every displayed EEG foundation-model reference on nine
datasets, including CHB-MIT, TUEV, ISRUC, FACED, PhysioNet-MI, and BCIC2020-3.
The result is not tied to one encoder: ViT-Small is strongest on CHB-MIT, TUEV,
and BCIC2020-3; ConvNeXt-Tiny on ISRUC, HMC, FACED, and PhysioNet-MI; and
EfficientNet-B0 on MentalArithmetic. Thus the interface transfers across both
CNN and Transformer backbones, although backbone choice remains task-dependent.

The comparison also defines clear limits. REVE remains strongest on TUAB and
Mumtaz2016, while CBraMod leads on SEED-V and SHU-MI for at least one metric;
visual transfer is therefore competitive, not a universal replacement for EEG
pretraining. PhysioNet-MI further shows that competitive transfer can occur at
$P=1$, without folding. The TUEV release difference leaves the held-out test
set unchanged. Detailed supervised controls are in Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results};
published foundation-model rows are contextual references rather than local
reruns, while matched-pipeline reruns of the released CBraMod and REVE
checkpoints under our protocol are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:foundation-rerun}.

representations provide a reusable initialization for EEG fine-tuning after
geometry alignment. This is evidence for transfer from visual pretraining to
EEG, not evidence that EEG pretraining is unnecessary in frozen, few-shot, or
cross-montage settings.

\subsection{Frozen transfer and linear probing}
\label{sec:linear-probe}

We freeze the DINOv3-initialized ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small encoders and
train only a linear task head to test direct representation transfer.
EfficientNet-B0 is excluded because its BatchNorm running statistics introduce
an additional choice between freezing and updating state.
Table~\ref{tab:linear-probe-representative} reports representative BA results;
Appendix~\ref{app:linear-probe} provides the complete 12-dataset comparison
against the published non-pooling references.

Frozen ConvNeXt exceeds the published non-pooling CBraMod probe on TUAB,
ISRUC, and MentalArithmetic, but remains below REVE-Base on those tasks.
FACED is backbone-sensitive, and both visual probes trail the published
references on BCIC2020-3. These results support task-dependent transfer from
visual representations without encoder updates, rather than universal
superiority or equivalence to EEG-specific pretraining. Linear probing alone
does not isolate the contribution of pretraining from architecture; the
matched initialization controls in Section~\ref{sec:pretraining-ablation}
address that question under full fine-tuning.

\subsection{Temporal folding versus contiguous reshape}
\label{sec:fold-reshape}

To isolate temporal adjacency from simple height expansion, we compare
temporal folding with a contiguous-chunk reshape. Both produce the
same $CP\times(T/P)$ tensor and retain the same samples; only their time-to-row
mapping differs. Across the three-backbone controls, temporal folding is favored
in 25 of 27 primary-metric comparisons (17 of 18 CNN and 8 of 9 ViT
comparisons), with small reversals on SHU-MI for EfficientNet-B0 and on CHB-MIT
for ViT-Small. The clearest gains occur on FACED and MentalArithmetic, with
smaller but consistent advantages on the clinical and sleep tasks; ViT-Small
shows its largest gains on SHU-MI and HMC.
Table~\ref{tab:fold-representative}
shows representative primary-metric values for the two CNN backbones; the
complete backbone-specific results are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:fold-reshape}.
Thus the comparison tests the
ordering of samples presented to the backbone, not merely the increase in
input height.

\subsection{Fold-factor sensitivity}
\label{sec:fold-factor-sensitivity}

The fold factor is tunable. We sweep $P\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ on CHB-MIT, TUEV,
and MentalArithmetic across all three backbones. The geometry-based rule
usually selects a factor near the best-performing region in these sweeps,
although the preferred value varies with the dataset, backbone, and metric.
We therefore use the rule as a reproducible geometric prior rather than a
universal optimum; validation-based tuning may provide additional gains.
Complete results are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:p-ablation}, and any tuned
factor uses validation data only.

\subsection{Model complexity}
\label{sec:model-complexity}

Table~\ref{tab:model-scale} compares encoder parameters and forward compute
on a shared $32\times1000$ EEG input with $P=4$ for visual models.
EfficientNet-B0 uses 4.01M parameters and 0.247 GFLOPs, compared with 4.92M
and 0.79 GFLOPs for \cbramod{} and
69.19M and 11.07 GFLOPs for \reve{}-Base. ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small require
2.903 and 3.000 GFLOPs, respectively. Folding introduces no learned parameters
or arithmetic operations, but requires $O(CT)$ memory movement. These counts
characterize model complexity rather than measured runtime or memory use.
