The unified table makes the central comparison explicit without averaging
incommensurate metrics across datasets. On BCIC2020-3, all three visual
backbones exceed the displayed EEG references on both reported metrics.
ConvNeXt-Tiny also exceeds the references on FACED and PhysioNet-MI, whereas
the completed visual runs trail the strongest EEG reference on TUAB,
SEED-V, and Mumtaz2016. The retained BA values in the appendix show both consistent gains and
task-specific trade-offs. The TUEV release difference does not change the
held-out test set used for comparison.
On PhysioNet-MI, EfficientNet-B0 and ConvNeXt-Tiny exceed both \cbramod{}
and \reve{} on $\kappa$ and weighted F1 with $P=1$, showing that competitive
visual transfer also occurs without folding.

The detailed tables in Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results} retain EEGNet,
SPaRCNet, BIOT, LaBraM-Base, and the other published supervised controls. They are
included to anchor the scale of the result, while the main text uses CBraMod
and REVE as the two foundation-model references rather than treating every
published architecture as a separate narrative claim.

\subsection{Cross-backbone consistency}
\label{sec:cross-backbone-main}

The completed DINOv3 probes extend the visual-transfer result beyond one CNN
family. The detailed per-dataset tables report ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small
alongside EfficientNet-B0 under the same folding interface, allowing
architecture and initialization to vary while the EEG representation remains
fixed. ConvNeXt-Tiny is strongest on most completed multiclass tasks,
including TUEV, ISRUC, FACED, and PhysioNet-MI, while also giving the best
visual result on CHB-MIT. EfficientNet-B0 remains competitive despite its
smaller encoder and is the strongest visual model on MentalArithmetic.
ViT-Small is competitive on TUEV and BCIC2020-3, but more task-sensitive,
falling behind on FACED and PhysioNet-MI. These results show that transfer is
not tied to one CNN family or ImageNet initialization, while backbone choice
still affects accuracy under the shared EEG--vision interface.

Pretraining improves balanced accuracy in 11 of the 12 matched comparisons,
by 0.4--24.6 percentage points
(Table~\ref{tab:vision-pretraining-ablation}). The one exception is SHU-MI
with ViT-Small, where random initialization lands within one point of
pretraining; more generally, the smallest gains appear at the smallest model
scale on the largest training set, where random-initialized EfficientNet-B0
is within 0.4 points of ImageNet pretraining on ISRUC. Thus generic visual
representations provide a reusable initialization for EEG fine-tuning after
geometry alignment, and the pretraining benefit grows with model capacity.
This is evidence for transfer from visual pretraining to EEG, not evidence
that EEG pretraining is unnecessary in frozen, few-shot, or cross-montage
settings.

\subsection{Phase folding versus contiguous reshape}
\label{sec:fold-reshape}

To isolate temporal adjacency from simple height expansion, we compare
phase-interleaved folding with a contiguous-chunk reshape. Both produce the
same $CP\times(T/P)$ tensor and retain the same samples; only their time-to-row
mapping differs. Across the completed EfficientNet-B0 controls, phase folding
improves the primary metric on most $P>1$ datasets, with the clearest gains on
FACED and MentalArithmetic; CHB-MIT is close and SHU-MI is the main exception.
Thus the comparison tests the ordering of samples presented to the backbone,
not merely the increase in input height. Full results are in
Appendix~\ref{app:fold-reshape}.

The fold factor is itself tunable. Across the evaluated sweeps, the
geometry-based default lies near the best-performing region despite using no
task-specific test-set tuning. The preferred value nevertheless varies with
task, backbone, and metric: larger factors are favored for CHB-MIT, whereas
TUEV and MentalArithmetic favor different settings across backbones. This
suggests that validation-based per-dataset selection of $P$ could yield
additional gains. We therefore use the geometry rule as a reproducible default
rather than a universal optimum, and select any tuned value using validation
data only. Complete sweeps are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:p-ablation}.

\subsection{Stability and model scale}

The balanced-accuracy standard deviations reveal two regimes. ISRUC (.0027)
and TUAB (.0060) are especially stable, while CHB-MIT (.0599) and
MentalArithmetic (.0418) remain seed-sensitive. MentalArithmetic has only
1,343 training examples and a roughly 3:1 class imbalance; its reported
.7903 PR-AUC is the validation-selected mean, not an exploratory best epoch.

After removing the unused ImageNet classifier, EfficientNet-B0 contains about
4.01M encoder parameters. The released \cbramod{} implementation contains
4.92M (its paper reports about 4.0M). At the shared $32\times1000$ input,
EfficientNet-B0 needs only 0.25 GFLOPs, versus 0.79 GFLOPs for \cbramod{}
and 11.07 GFLOPs for the 69.19M-parameter \reve{}-Base; ConvNeXt-Tiny and
ViT-Small require 2.90 and 3.00 GFLOPs (Table~\ref{tab:model-scale}).
Folding itself adds no parameters and no arithmetic: it is a lossless index
permutation with $O(CT)$ memory movement. \reve{}-Base is much larger and
pretrained on a broader EEG corpus. The comparison therefore isolates a
narrower conclusion: at compact scale and under full fine-tuning, input
geometry and generic visual features can account for a large fraction of
downstream performance. It does not test the frozen-transfer regime where
large EEG foundation models may retain a larger advantage.
