The unified tables make the central comparison explicit without averaging
incommensurate metrics across datasets. On BCIC2020-3, all three visual
backbones exceed the displayed EEG references on both reported metrics.
ConvNeXt-Tiny also exceeds the references on FACED and PhysioNet-MI, whereas
the completed visual runs trail the strongest EEG reference on TUAB,
SEED-V, and Mumtaz2016. The retained BA values in the appendix show both consistent gains and
task-specific trade-offs. The cross-version
TUEV comparison and the preprocessing difference on REVE--ISRUC remain
explicit caveats.

The detailed tables in Appendix~\ref{app:detailed-results} retain EEGNet,
SPaRCNet, BIOT, LaBraM-Base, and the other published supervised controls. They are
included to anchor the scale of the result, while the main text uses CBraMod
and REVE as the two foundation-model references rather than treating every
published architecture as a separate narrative claim.

\subsection{Cross-backbone consistency}
\label{sec:cross-backbone-main}

The completed DINOv3 probes extend the visual-transfer result beyond one CNN
family. Table~\ref{tab:cross-backbone} in the appendix reports every completed
ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small configuration; pending configurations are marked
as --. The same folding interface is used for all visual backbones, allowing
architecture and initialization to vary while the EEG representation remains
fixed. This is evidence that transfer is a property of the aligned
EEG--vision interface, rather than an EfficientNet-specific effect.

\subsection{Representative behavior}

ISRUC gives stable folded-input results: EfficientNet-B0 reaches
$\kappa=.7705\pm.0032$ across five seeds. The result uses $P=12$ and retains
20 consecutive sleep epochs, so the
adapter also works when one record carries multiple labels. PhysioNet-MI uses
$P=1$ and still improves over both published references on $\kappa$ and
weighted F1, showing that the transfer effect is not caused by folding alone.
FACED is architecture-sensitive rather than uniformly unfavorable to visual
transfer. ConvNeXt-Tiny reaches $\kappa=.5891$, above both published EEG
foundation baselines, whereas EfficientNet-B0 is close to them and ViT-Small
is substantially lower. The result shows that performance with the shared
interface depends on the backbone and its training recipe.

Pretraining improves $\kappa$ in all six matched comparisons, by 7.48--27.82
percentage points. The same direction holds for the retained auxiliary
metrics: on TUEV, pretrained initialization improves BA by 10.95, 24.61, and
24.30 points and weighted F1 by 4.66, 10.79, and 13.62 points for
EfficientNet, ConvNeXt, and ViT, respectively. On PhysioNet-MI, the
corresponding BA gains are 5.60, 20.86, and 9.08 points, while weighted-F1
gains are 5.13, 20.89, and 9.07 points. Thus the result is not explained by
architecture capacity alone: generic visual representations provide a
reusable initialization for EEG fine-tuning after geometry alignment. It is
evidence for transfer from visual pretraining to EEG, not evidence that EEG
pretraining is unnecessary in frozen, few-shot, or cross-montage settings.

\subsection{Stability and model scale}

The balanced-accuracy standard deviations reveal two regimes. ISRUC (.0027)
and TUAB (.0060) are especially stable, while CHB-MIT (.0599) and
MentalArithmetic (.0418) remain seed-sensitive. MentalArithmetic has only
1,343 training examples and a roughly 3:1 class imbalance; its reported
.7903 PR-AUC is the validation-selected mean, not an exploratory best epoch.

After removing the unused ImageNet classifier, EfficientNet-B0 contains about
4.01M encoder parameters, close to the reported 4.0M parameters of
\cbramod{}. \reve{}-Base is much larger and pretrained on a broader EEG
corpus. The comparison therefore isolates a narrower conclusion: at compact
scale and under full fine-tuning, input geometry and generic visual features
can account for a large fraction of downstream performance. It does not test
the frozen-transfer regime where large EEG foundation models may retain a
larger advantage.
