\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a phase-interleaved, lossless temporal-folding interface between
raw EEG and generic vision backbones.  The method changes no waveform values
and adds no learned adapter parameters, yet it substantially changes the
neighborhoods and downsampling trajectory seen by a 2D CNN.  Across 12
benchmarks, EfficientNet-B0 is competitive with the displayed supervised and
EEG-foundation baselines on several tasks. ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-Small extend
the comparison to additional visual architectures: ConvNeXt-Tiny leads the
displayed FACED results, while the strongest EEG reference remains ahead of
the completed visual runs on TUAB, SEED-V, and Mumtaz2016. The
uniform BA/PR-AUC/ROC-AUC and BA/$\kappa$/F1 reporting makes these trade-offs
visible without collapsing unlike metrics into one score. The pattern supports
competitiveness, not universal superiority, and still requires same-pipeline
foundation-model reproduction for a paired claim.

The broader message is methodological: before attributing downstream accuracy
to large-scale EEG pretraining, representation geometry must be treated as a
first-class baseline variable.  The matched five-seed initialization controls
on TUEV and PhysioNet-MI show that ImageNet/DINOv3 visual weights improve all
three tested architectures over their random-initialized counterparts, giving
direct evidence that visual pretraining can transfer to EEG after geometry
alignment.  Further same-split foundation-model comparisons and topology-aware experiments
can test how far this observation extends.  In this sense,
vision pretraining is not a universal free lunch, but a strong reusable prior
whose benefit becomes visible when EEG geometry is matched to the inductive
bias of the encoder.
