\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a phase-interleaved, lossless temporal-folding interface between
raw EEG and generic vision backbones.  The method changes no waveform values
and adds no learned adapter parameters, yet it substantially changes the
neighborhoods and downsampling trajectory seen by a 2D CNN.  Across 11
benchmarks, EfficientNet-B0 exceeds published \cbramod{} primary metrics on
six datasets and trails on five. The expanded foundation-model comparison
places it first among the displayed methods on five
tasks; across the eight REVE-overlap tasks it is higher on four, with explicit
version or preprocessing caveats on two. The pattern supports competitiveness,
not universal superiority, and still requires same-pipeline foundation-model
reproduction for a paired claim.

The broader message is methodological: before attributing downstream accuracy
to large-scale EEG pretraining, representation geometry must be treated as a
first-class baseline variable.  The matched five-seed initialization controls
on TUEV and PhysioNet-MI show that ImageNet/DINOv3 visual weights improve all
three tested architectures over their random-initialized counterparts, giving
direct evidence that visual pretraining can transfer to EEG after geometry
alignment.  Completing the same-split foundation-model reproductions,
prospective evaluation, and topology-aware extension will determine when this
observation matures into a general EEG learning principle.  In this sense,
vision pretraining is not a universal free lunch, but a strong reusable prior
whose benefit becomes visible when EEG geometry is matched to the inductive
bias of the encoder.
