\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmarks and split provenance}

The suite covers 12 datasets and six broad application categories: clinical
detection (CHB-MIT, TUAB, and TUEV), sleep staging (ISRUC and HMC), emotion recognition
(FACED and SEED-V), motor imagery (PhysioNet-MI and SHU-MI), imagined speech
(BCIC2020-3), depression detection (Mumtaz2016), and mental workload
(MentalArithmetic).  Table~\ref{tab:datasets} reports the exact shapes and split
sizes used by the local pipeline.  All regular datasets satisfy the
$[B,C,T]$ contract.  ISRUC retains 20 consecutive epochs so that training and
evaluation operate on all labels in the record.

The source collections include CHB-MIT \citep{shoeb2009application}, the TUH
corpora underlying TUAB and TUEV \citep{obeid2016temple}, ISRUC-Sleep
\citep{khalighi2016isruc}, FACED \citep{chen2023large}, the PhysioNet motor
movement/imagery recordings distributed through BCI2000
\citep{schalk2004bci2000}, SEED-V \citep{liu2021comparing}, SHU-MI
\citep{ma2022large}, BCIC2020-3 \citep{jeong20222020}, Mumtaz2016
\citep{mumtaz2016mdd}, MentalArithmetic \citep{zyma2019mental}, and the HMC
sleep-staging corpus \citep{alvarez2022hmc}.

CHB-MIT segments are resampled to 2,000 samples in the dataloader. Across the
full suite, we inherit the public split definitions of \cbramod{} and \reve{}
and retain the same train, validation, and test partitions used in their
benchmark evaluations \citep{wang2024cbramod,elouahidi2025reve}. For example,
CHB-MIT uses CHB01--20 for training, CHB21--22 for validation, and CHB23--24
for testing, following the \cbramod{} segmentation code. Eight tasks are
shared by both reference papers and our study (TUAB, TUEV, ISRUC, FACED,
PhysioNet-MI, BCIC2020-3, Mumtaz2016, and MentalArithmetic); CHB-MIT, SEED-V,
and SHU-MI are covered by \cbramod{} but not \reve{}, whereas HMC is covered
by \reve{} but not \cbramod{}. Every dataset therefore inherits a fixed
partition from at least one of the two benchmark codebases, and the
\cbramod{} and \reve{} rows share the held-out partitions of our runs. Our
TUEV files are prepared from v2.0.1 rather than the v2.0.0 release used by the
two reference benchmarks: the held-out partition is the official TUEV
evaluation set and is identical in both releases (29,421 segments), while
v2.0.1 changes only the training and validation segments (68,445/15,487
rather than 67,436/15,634).

\subsection{Training and implementation}

All models use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled} with cosine decay,
bfloat16 mixed precision, dropout 0.1, and batch size 32 except ISRUC (16) and
MentalArithmetic (64). The ImageNet/DINOv3 backbone is fine-tuned jointly with
a newly initialized linear head, and bias and normalization parameters receive
zero weight decay. Samples are converted to float32, clipped, and scaled
before folding (default $x\mapsto x/32$ after clipping to $[-1024,1024]$), and
the fold factor is fixed before training using Eq.~\ref{eq:p-geometry-rule}.
Dataset-specific learning rates, weight decay, epochs, EMA settings, and
augmentations are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:recipes}; seeds, checkpoint
selection, and the final-test protocol follow Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\paragraph{Initialization ablation.}
To separate visual pretraining from the encoder architecture itself, we run
matched five-seed controls on TUEV, PhysioNet-MI, ISRUC, and SHU-MI for
EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt-Tiny DINOv3, and ViT-Small DINOv3. Each control
keeps the dataset recipe, folding, optimizer, epoch budget, validation
checkpoint selection, and one-final-test protocol fixed, changing only the
initialization from the available ImageNet/DINOv3 weights to random
initialization (``\texttt{use\_pretrained\_weights=false}''), so the
comparison tests whether the generic visual prior contributes beyond the
capacity and inductive bias of the same architecture.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

The result tables retain the published comparison context (EEGNet,
EEGConformer, SPaRCNet, ContraWR, CNN-Transformer, FFCL, ST-Transformer, BIOT,
LaBraM, \cbramod{}, and \reve{}-Base). Published values, including the
\cbramod{} TUAB and TUEV target-corpus-excluded controls, are transcribed from
the source benchmark tables \citep{wang2024cbramod,elouahidi2025reve}; our
local rows are validation-selected repeated-seed runs. The main interpretation
emphasizes \cbramod{}, \reve{}-Base, and \method{} because they isolate the
paper's central foundation-model question, while the remaining rows stay
visible as architecture controls.

Binary tasks report balanced accuracy (BA), PR-AUC, and ROC-AUC; multiclass
tasks report BA, Cohen's $\kappa$, and weighted F1. Checkpoint selection uses
only validation PR-AUC or validation $\kappa$, while the remaining metrics are
test-time reporting metrics; published BAs are transcribed from the same
benchmark tables and are never mixed across runs. Each recipe is trained with
five seeds (42--46), and we report the arithmetic mean and population standard
deviation after selecting one checkpoint on validation and evaluating it once
on test. The executable protocol is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.
