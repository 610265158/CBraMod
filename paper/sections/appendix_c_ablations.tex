\paragraph{Discussion.}
Across the matched initialization tables (Tables~\ref{tab:app-init-effnet}--\ref{tab:app-init-vit}), visual pretraining is most beneficial
for TUEV, FACED, MentalArithmetic, and BCIC2020-3, while gains are smaller or
mixed on ISRUC, TUAB, and Mumtaz2016. The pattern also depends on the backbone: ConvNeXt-Tiny
and ViT-Small often show larger improvements than EfficientNet-B0, whereas some
tasks show little or negative change. These results support a transferable
visual initialization, but not a uniform replacement for EEG pretraining.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The temporal-folding control shows a consistent advantage for CNN backbones across
most datasets, with the largest differences on FACED and MentalArithmetic and
smaller but positive differences on clinical and sleep tasks (the only CNN
exception is a 0.3pp SHU-MI reversal for EfficientNet-B0). ViT-Small is more
task-sensitive: it benefits clearly on TUEV, HMC, and SHU-MI, but contiguous
reshape is better on CHB-MIT and nearly tied on FACED and MentalArithmetic.
Thus the gain is not explained by height expansion alone; it depends on how the
backbone uses the phase-structured neighborhoods.

The sweeps confirm that the preferred factor is task- and backbone-dependent.
On MentalArithmetic, EfficientNet-B0 peaks at $P=4$ whereas both DINOv3
backbones peak at $P=2$ on PR-AUC. On CHB-MIT, PR-AUC is best at $P=4$ for
EfficientNet-B0 and ViT-Small but at $P=1$ for ConvNeXt-Tiny, and
EfficientNet-B0 reaches its highest BA at $P=8$ without improving its
validation-selected metric. On TUEV, $\kappa$ and weighted F1 are best at
$P=8$ for ConvNeXt-Tiny and at $P=4$ for ViT-Small, while EfficientNet-B0 is
nearly tied between $P=2$ and $P=4$ ($\kappa=.6907$ vs $.6901$). The geometry
rule therefore serves as a reproducible default; validation-based tuning can
improve the result when additional compute is available.
