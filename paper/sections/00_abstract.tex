\begin{abstract}
EEG foundation models are widely used for general-purpose decoding, but their
reported gains are difficult to separate from backbone capacity and input
geometry. We ask whether visual representations learned without EEG data can
transfer to EEG through a suitable interface. We introduce lossless phase-interleaved temporal folding, which rearranges a
channel--time EEG segment into a two-dimensional tensor without discarding
samples. This representation allows standard convolutional and Transformer
vision backbones to process EEG directly. Across 12 downstream benchmarks,
ImageNet- and DINOv3-initialized EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and ViT-Small
models match or exceed the strongest available EEG foundation reference on 8
of the 12 tasks. Matched random-initialization controls further show
that pretrained visual weights contribute substantially to these gains. Our results establish lossless folding with visual pretraining as a strong and
reproducible EEG baseline, while leaving open the advantages of EEG-specific
pretraining for frozen, few-shot, and cross-montage transfer.
\end{abstract}
