\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Task-specific decoders and EEG foundation models}

Early EEG decoders established convolution as a useful inductive bias.
EEGNet combines compact temporal filtering with depthwise spatial convolution
\citep{lawhern2018eegnet}, while DeepConvNet and ShallowConvNet stack temporal
filters, spatial filters, and pooling like a learned signal-processing
pipeline \citep{schirrmeister2017deep}. Such models remain strong on their
target benchmarks but are trained from scratch and assume a fixed channel
layout, limiting transfer across datasets, montages, and label spaces.

EEG foundation models address this limitation by scaling self-supervised
pretraining. BIOT maps heterogeneous biosignals to frequency-domain tokens
\citep{yang2024biot}; LaBraM applies masked EEG modeling at scale
\citep{jiang2024labram}; CBraMod combines time-domain patches, Fourier
magnitude, and criss--cross attention in a compact encoder
\citep{wang2024cbramod}; and REVE incorporates physical electrode coordinates
for large-scale pretraining and unseen montages \citep{elouahidi2025reve}.

These methods change pretraining data, objective, backbone, tokenization, and
input geometry simultaneously, so it remains unclear how much of the observed
improvement is attributable to EEG pretraining. Recent work raises a related
concern by showing that carefully tuned conventional decoders remain
competitive across diverse BCI tasks \citep{yang2026eegworth}. We provide a
complementary control: remove EEG pretraining, retain a mature visual backbone
and a lossless geometry adapter, and test whether generic visual
representations transfer when EEG is presented in a compatible form.

\subsection{Vision transfer and lossless rearrangement}

ImageNet pretraining provides transferable low- and mid-level filters
\citep{deng2009imagenet}. EfficientNet jointly scales width, depth, and
resolution \citep{tan2019efficientnet}, and vision transformers offer a
patch-token alternative \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit}. Generic vision models have
been applied to spectrograms and topographic EEG maps, but those
representations introduce a Fourier transform, interpolation, or an electrode
layout; our input remains the raw waveform and changes indices only.

Pixel shuffle and space-to-depth operations show that a tensor reindexing can
substantially alter the computation of a fixed convolutional architecture
\citep{shi2016realtime}. Temporal folding is related in spirit but asymmetric:
it trades temporal width for a joint electrode--phase height, with each folded
column holding $P$ consecutive samples and each row following one phase at
stride $P$. This explicit signal meaning lets us analyze the original temporal
offsets seen by local 2D neighbors.

\subsection{Positioning of this work}

The closest empirical references are \cbramod{} and \reve{}, whose benchmark
suites supply the subject- or trial-level partitions used here and which
expose two complementary EEG priors: a compact criss-cross transformer over
temporal and cross-channel structure versus physical electrode coordinates
with 69M parameters pretrained on 92 datasets. We do not propose another EEG
foundation model; we ask how far a 4.01M-parameter vision-pretrained encoder
can go after only a lossless change of input geometry, separating three
sources of downstream performance---task architecture, EEG pretraining, and
representation geometry---that are otherwise easy to conflate.
